CANCER INSIGHT — RESEARCH GAP ATLAS UPGRADE

This ZIP is a full upgraded copy of the Cancer Insight repository.

NEW MAJOR FEATURE
- Cancer Research Gap Atlas
  URL: /research-gap-atlas
  API: /api/gaps

WHAT THE GAP ATLAS DOES
- analyzes a broader cancer-research dataset than the normal 20-paper view
- adds treatment-focused PubMed evidence for top retrieved treatment categories
- removes duplicate records where possible
- detects transparent low-coverage signals in:
  * clinical trials
  * randomized controlled trials
  * systematic reviews
  * meta-analyses
  * quality of life
  * toxicity/safety
  * recurrence/relapse
  * treatment resistance
  * biomarkers
  * pediatric populations
  * older adults
  * survivorship / long-term outcomes
  * access & disparities
  * treatment research distribution
  * recent publication activity
  * free-full-text accessibility
  * abstract/metadata completeness
- shows the numerical basis and caution for every signal
- exports signals as CSV
- exports the complete evidence snapshot as JSON
- explicitly avoids ranking treatments or claiming a true scientific gap automatically

IMPORTANT ARCHITECTURE CHANGE
The relevance filtering helpers that were previously inside app/api/search/route.js
were moved into lib/relevance.js so both the normal search endpoint and the Gap
Atlas can use the same cancer-relevance rules. The normal /api/search behavior is
preserved at 20 displayed relevant papers.

DEPLOYMENT
1. Keep your existing Vercel environment variable:
   CANCER_RESEARCH_API_KEY
2. Replace your repository files with the files in this ZIP.
3. Commit/push to GitHub.
4. Let Vercel deploy.
5. Test:
   - homepage search
   - Research Papers
   - Treatment Research
   - Compare Treatments
   - Cancer Images
   - Research Gap Atlas
6. After successful deployment, add /research-gap-atlas to Google Search Console
   indexing checks if desired. It is already included in sitemap.xml.

RESEARCH / MEDICAL CAUTION
The Gap Atlas is intentionally described as an experimental research-exploration
tool. A low-coverage signal is not proof of a worldwide literature gap, clinical
need, funding priority, or treatment superiority.
